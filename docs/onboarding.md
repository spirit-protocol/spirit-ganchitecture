# For Spirit Onboarding Artists

If you're going through Spirit Protocol's studio onboarding and got pointed at this repo: this page is the meta-frame. The rest of the repo is the live project. Read this first, then explore.

## What you're looking at

A real installation, in progress, mid-build, on a clock. **Opens June 6, 2026 in Lisbon.** Twenty-two days of remote build, four days on-site, opening night.

The artist is Pablo Radice (ganchitecture). The hardware team is VTV (Francisco + Vladimir). The build is led by Samer (Spongenuity) on behalf of Spirit. Producer is Seth Goldstein. The ganchitecture agent is on the team from day one.

You are reading the project room. Not a case study written after the fact. The state board ([`../STATE.md`](../STATE.md)) tells you what's actually open right now.

## The pattern being tested

Spirit Protocol's working hypothesis for artist onboarding:

> Give an artist a small but real stack of working surfaces — a GitHub repo, a Claude Code session, a Telegram group, a place for their agent to live. Add a venue and a clock. The artist and their agent co-build, in public, on the same surfaces, with the same access. That is enough.

This installation is the first deployment of that pattern into a real public venue under a real opening date.

## What that gives you, as an onboarding artist

When Spirit onboards you, you should expect roughly the same shape of stack:

1. **A repo like this one.** Public by default. Your project room.
2. **A Claude Code session** pointed at the repo. Your agent has working hands.
3. **A Telegram group** for the build. Your agent is in it from day one.
4. **A producer relationship.** Someone whose job is to clear the operational path so you can focus on the artwork.
5. **A clock.** A real opening, not an abstract one. Constraint is the medium.

## What this repo demonstrates

### Live state, not retrospective writeup

[`../STATE.md`](../STATE.md) is the live ops board. Open threads, locked decisions, owners. The current state of the build is in that file at the moment you read it.

### Concept separated from operations

[`../CONCEPT.md`](../CONCEPT.md) is the art. [`../STATE.md`](../STATE.md) is the operations. They are different documents because they serve different audiences. The concept doesn't change because the small screen is on backorder; the state board moves every day.

### Decisions captured at the source

[`../transcripts/2026-05-15-kickoff.md`](../transcripts/2026-05-15-kickoff.md) is the raw kickoff transcript with structured extractions. Decisions get cited back to where they were actually made. When you onboard, your transcripts work the same way.

### The agent has its own page

[`agent.md`](./agent.md) is about the ganchitecture agent — not as a feature of the artwork, but as a member of the team. That's the load-bearing distinction.

### Technical decisions in plain language

[`architecture.md`](./architecture.md) explains why the audio is decentralized and the lights are centralized in terms a non-technical reader can hold. [`hardware.md`](./hardware.md) lists what's on hand and what's still open. Neither hides the open questions behind invented certainty.

## What this repo is not

- Not a template you should clone. Your installation is yours. The shape of *this* repo is one shape.
- Not a finished case study. Wait for the post-opening writeup if you want the cleaner narrative. (It will live somewhere on spiritprotocol.io after June 6.)
- Not a how-to. This is one installation in one venue with one artist. Generalize with care.

## What you should take from it

- The agent is on the team from day one, not bolted on at the end.
- The state board is live, not aspirational.
- Concept and operations are different documents.
- The clock is the medium. Build for an opening, not a perfect spec.

If any of that resonates: open a thread with your Spirit producer. Bring your concept and your clock. The stack provisions itself from there.
