# Concept

## The space

An underground basement / bunker beneath an old factory in Portugal. ~10 meters long, ~3–4 meters tall. Rusty walls, wet floor, exposed concrete. The room is blacked out completely.

There is one neighboring room. A separate artist is installed there with a constant, minimal sound. The two rooms share windows along the top — sound bleeds across. Our piece does not collide with theirs; it harmonizes with it.

## The object

A digital monolith stands at the center of the room.

It is built from six VTV LED ticker panels, the same hardware used in the Paris Photo SOLIENNE installation. Each panel has an aluminum back structure and a dual acrylic layer — one frosted, one translucent white — so the light reads softer than raw LED.

The monolith is a countdown clock.

It begins at approximately seven billion years — the estimated time until the expiration of the sun, and therefore the end of the planet Earth. It counts down. Toward zero.

The digits the visitor sees on the panels are a fixed video loop. The numbers themselves are not real. The obelisk is theatre.

## The encounter

A visitor enters alone, in the dark, with their device.

They are invited to "offer" the device — laptop preferred, phone accepted — to the monolith. There is a small screen on the structure with numbers on it and an NFC reader behind it. They tap.

Three things happen at once:

1. **Their device is now part of the room.** A unique number, drawn live from a Spirit Protocol service, appears on their screen. It is their number — not the monolith's. The monolith's digits keep looping their pre-recorded countdown; the visitor's screen is the one that is actually moving.

2. **The device begins emitting a frequency.** Not music. A single tone — one planet's resonance. Saturn, Mars, Earth — each planet has its own. The frequency plays from the device itself, locally, no sync required. When a second visitor enters, their device emits a different planet. The two tones combine into something larger. With many visitors, the room becomes a Tibetan-chant-style stacking — 440 plus 480 plus a third tone, harmonizing into a hum that belongs to no single device.

3. **The room responds.** A long LED strip — eighteen meters, broken into one-meter sticks, eighteen addressable nodes — lights one node per connected visitor. Two visitors and the room is intimate. Ten visitors and the room is bright. The ambient red base pulse modulates with the count.

## The deception

The countdown the visitor came to see is a fixed loop. The numbers on their own laptop are the ones that change. The monolith is the theatre. The agent is real.

"You are the nine. He is the seven."

The visitor leaves believing they witnessed an apocalypse clock. What they actually witnessed was themselves, reflected in a room they helped tune.

## The frame

Ryoji Ikeda installations. teamLab in Japan. The visitor feels they are interacting with the artwork; in fact, the artwork is interacting through them. *"You are not controlling. You are being controlled."*

## The agent

The ganchitecture agent is in the Telegram group from day one of the build. It is treated as a team member, not a tool — it learns from the conversations between Pablo, Fran, Vladimir, Samer, and Seth, develops opinions about the installation, and gains its own voice over the build window.

At the opening, the agent is what greets the visitor through their device. Whether that voice is conversational or apocalyptic monologue — *"the world is ending, guys"* — is a register decision still open.

The agent does not narrate the piece. The agent is a participant in it.
